# Amazon FBA — German Market Facts

Reference for any article written for the German `/de/` section. Numbers are accurate to the best of my knowledge as of 2026-04-27 — flag in `[REVIEW]` blocks anything time-sensitive (VAT rates, fee thresholds, government reg requirements) and have a German Steuerberater or active DE seller verify before publishing under TheFBAGirl's brand.

## Mehrwertsteuer (VAT) — the basics every DE seller must know

- **Standard rate (Regelsatz)**: 19 % USt. — applied to most goods sold on Amazon.de.
- **Reduced rate (ermäßigt)**: 7 % — books, food staples, certain medical, cultural goods. Most FBA SKUs fall under 19 %.
- **Kleinunternehmerregelung (§ 19 UStG)**: optional. If annual turnover < 25.000 € in current year and < 100.000 € in prior year (raised from 22.000 / 50.000 thresholds in 2025), seller may opt out of charging VAT. Most serious FBA sellers do NOT use it because they exceed thresholds quickly and lose Vorsteuer-Abzug. Mention it in beginner content but advise checking with a Steuerberater.
- **Vorsteuer-Abzug**: input VAT deduction — sellers who charge VAT can claim back VAT on business purchases (inventory, ads, software). Major reason most sellers don't use Kleinunternehmerregelung once volume grows.
- **Reverse-Charge**: when buying business services from another EU country (e.g. Amazon EU S.à r.l. invoicing from Luxembourg), the buyer pays VAT in their own country. Steuerberater handles this; sellers should know the term.

## OSS (One-Stop-Shop) — central concept post-2021

- Replaces the old country-by-country distance-selling registrations within the EU.
- Threshold: 10.000 € annual cross-border B2C sales across EU. Once crossed, seller charges destination-country VAT and reports/pays via the OSS portal in their home country (in DE: BZSt — Bundeszentralamt für Steuern).
- **OSS does NOT replace local VAT registrations in countries where you store inventory.** This is the most-misunderstood point. If FBA stores your inventory in France via Pan-EU, you need a French VAT number — OSS doesn't help.
- Quarterly filing.

## Pan-EU FBA vs CEE vs EFN

Three different fulfillment programs — sellers conflate them:
- **EFN (European Fulfilment Network)**: store in one country (typically DE), Amazon ships to other EU countries from that one warehouse. No additional VAT registrations needed (just OSS for cross-border B2C). But: cross-border fulfillment fees are higher, and inventory limits are tighter.
- **CEE (Central Europe Programme)**: PL + CZ added as storage countries on top of DE. Requires VAT registrations in PL + CZ. Lower fulfillment fees vs EFN, faster delivery.
- **Pan-EU FBA**: Amazon distributes inventory across DE, FR, IT, ES, PL, CZ (and now sometimes NL, SE depending on rollout). Requires VAT registration in all storage countries. Lowest per-unit fulfillment fees, fastest Prime delivery, BUT highest compliance burden — usually 5–7 VAT registrations + monthly Intrastat reporting in some countries.

**Steuerberater cost reality**: a competent Steuerberater handling 5-country Pan-EU compliance typically charges 250–500 €/month minimum. Some specialists (Hellotax, Taxdoo, Avalara) sell software-as-a-service to handle the VAT side at lower cost, with hand-off to a tax advisor for the year-end Bilanz.

## Verpackungsgesetz (VerpackG) — German packaging law

- Mandatory for any seller shipping packaged goods to German end customers — including foreign sellers.
- Register with **LUCID-Verpackungsregister** at zentrale-stelle-verpackungsregister.de. Free.
- Then license your packaging volumes via a system like **Der Grüne Punkt**, Reclay, Interzero — paid annually, costs scale with volume.
- Failure to register means Amazon can deactivate your listings on Amazon.de. Amazon now actively enforces this — a real seller pain point.

## Elektrogesetz (ElektroG / WEEE) and BattG

- **WEEE-Nummer**: required if you sell any electrical or electronic equipment. Register with `Stiftung Elektro-Altgeräte Register (Stiftung EAR)`. Many product categories trigger this — even some products sellers don't think of as „elektronisch" (LED lights, USB cables, anything battery-powered).
- **BattG**: Battery Act. Anyone selling products containing batteries (including loose AA batteries with a product) must register with BattG-Melderegister.
- Annual fees apply to both.

## EORI-Nummer (customs)

- Required for any business importing goods into the EU.
- Free to register; in DE done via the customs portal `zoll.de`.
- Format: `DE` + 12 digits.
- Without it, customs holds shipments and Amazon will mark inventory as „nicht lieferbar".

## Gewerbeanmeldung & Rechtsform

- Hobby-Verkauf endet schnell. Most German FBA sellers operate as either:
  - **Einzelunternehmen** (sole proprietor) — easiest, gets a `Steuernummer` from Finanzamt, registered via local Gewerbeamt for ~30 €.
  - **GmbH** — limited liability, ~25.000 € Stammkapital, IHK-pflichtig. Most sellers wait until profitable and then wechseln.
  - **UG (haftungsbeschränkt)** — „Mini-GmbH", 1 € Stammkapital. Common middle step.
- Tax burden differs significantly. Einzelunternehmer pays Einkommensteuer on profits; GmbH pays Körperschaftsteuer (~15 %) + Gewerbesteuer (varies by Hebesatz, typically 14–17 %). Total effective ~30 % vs. progressive Einkommensteuer up to 45 %.

## EU-Produktsicherheitsverordnung (GPSR) — relevant 2024+

- Came into force December 2024.
- Requires named „verantwortliche Person" within the EU on every product page and packaging — name, postal address, email.
- Foreign sellers without an EU entity need an `Authorised Representative` service. Several services exist (e.g. EU Rep providers).
- Amazon enforces — listings without GPSR-compliant info get suppressed.

## Important German FBA seller vocabulary

- **Versandkosten**: shipping costs (to seller, not customer — customers see them as included on Prime listings).
- **Lieferant**: supplier.
- **Großhändler**: wholesaler.
- **Hersteller**: manufacturer.
- **Markenanmeldung**: trademark filing — for Brand Registry, sellers usually file with DPMA (German trademark office) or EUIPO (EU-wide).
- **Markenregister**: trademark register.
- **Wareneingang**: inbound — Amazon's "shipment received" status.
- **Warenausgang**: outbound — order shipped.
- **Retoure**: return.
- **Bewertung**: review (a star rating). „Rezension" can also be used for written reviews.
- **Konto-Status / Kontoauszug**: account health dashboard.
- **Erstattung**: refund.
- **Auszahlung**: payout / disbursement from Amazon.
- **Kontostand**: account balance.

## Unique German FBA market dynamics

- **Smaller market than US**: Amazon.de has ~50–60 M active customers vs. ~200 M+ for Amazon.com. Reviews come slower (rule of thumb: 4–10× slower review velocity per sale than US).
- **Higher returns**: German consumer protection law (Fernabsatzgesetz) gives 14-day return without reason. Combined with cultural „lieber zurückschicken" mindset, return rates on apparel/shoes can hit 30–50 %. Hard goods generally lower (5–15 %).
- **Competition is dense in classic categories**: cookware, tools, electronics. New sellers underestimate this and get crushed in commoditized niches.
- **Pricing pressure**: Germans are price-sensitive. Same product often sells 10–20 % cheaper on Amazon.de vs Amazon.com after VAT-removed comparison.
- **Slower buying decisions**: longer time-on-listing, more comparison shopping. Implication: A+ Content and detailed bullets matter more, not less.
- **Trust signals matter more**: clear Hersteller info, real Markenname, EU return address — German buyers check these.

## What's the same as US

- PPC mechanics: Sponsored Products / Brands / Display, ACoS, bidding, search term reports — identical.
- Brand Registry: same global program; trademark must be valid in the marketplace's region (so EUIPO mark covers DE/FR/IT/ES/etc.; US trademark needed separately for amazon.com BR).
- Listing optimization principles: title structure, bullet hooks, A+ content — same playbook, just with German keyword research.
- Inventory management mechanics, IPI, restock limits — same systems.
