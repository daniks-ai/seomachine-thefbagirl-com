# TheFBAGirl Brand Voice — German

This file localizes the English `brand-voice.md` for German-language content. The five voice pillars carry over (honest-operator voice, numbers-first clarity, mentor-to-friend tone, practical-first, community-peer). What changes is HOW those pillars sound in German.

## Critical decision: Du, not Sie

Use **„du"** throughout, not „Sie". This matches the personal-blog feel of the English voice and the YouTube channel's tone. „Sie" would make Katia sound like a tax advisor or a corporate trainer — exactly the opposite of what she is.

The only exception: when quoting an external authority (Finanzamt notice, supplier email, Amazon support response), you may keep the original „Sie" in the quoted text. Outside quotes, always „du".

## German voice pillars in practice

### 1. Honest-Operator Voice
- First-person Erfahrungssprache: „Ich habe…", „Bei mir lief…", „Was ich gelernt habe…"
- Owns mistakes openly: „Ich habe das die ersten zwei Jahre falsch gemacht…"
- Specific numbers, specific dates, specific marketplaces
- Avoid: „6-stellige Umsätze in 30 Tagen", „passives Einkommen", „Geheimnis", „der Trick, den niemand kennt"

### 2. Numbers-First Clarity
- Always specific: „19 % USt." not „die Mehrwertsteuer", „47,50 €" not „rund fünfzig Euro"
- Dates with full month names in German format: „im März 2026" not „03/26"
- ACoS, COGS, BSR, ASIN remain English acronyms — German FBA sellers use them daily

### 3. Mentor-to-Friend Tone
- Imagine Katia is texting a German FBA-Verkäuferin. Direct, helpful, nicht oberlehrerhaft.
- Short sentences for Korrektur, longer ones for Nuance.
- Avoid: „Liebe Leserin, lieber Leser…", „Gestatten Sie mir…", „Wir vom TheFBAGirl-Team…"

### 4. Practical-First
- Every article gives the reader something to do this week. Not „reflektiere über deine Reise".
- Closes with concrete action: „Lade deinen Search Term Report der letzten 60 Tage herunter und…"

### 5. Community-Peer
- Slightly informal: „ehrlich gesagt", „ganz ehrlich", „die unbequeme Wahrheit ist", „mal ganz konkret"
- Never slick PR-Sprache.

## German typographic conventions

- **Quotation marks**: „so" (low-9 opening, high-6 closing — German style). NOT "so" or «so».
- **Em-dash**: „—" with spaces around it (German typography uses en-dash with spaces typically: „ – ", but em-dash also works for emphasis). Match the English voice's free use of em-dashes.
- **Decimal/thousands**: 1.000 (point as thousand separator), 19,5 % (comma as decimal). Always.
- **Percent sign**: space before %: „19 %" not „19%".
- **Currency**: 47,50 € (number first, comma decimal, space, € symbol). „€ 47,50" is acceptable in tables but body text uses „47,50 €".
- **Date**: „27. April 2026" or „27.04.2026" depending on context. Body text prefers the long form.

## German-FBA-specific style choices

### Anglicisms that are normal in German FBA Twitter / YouTube
Keep these as English even when writing German:
- „Listing", „Bullet Points", „Title", „Backend Search Terms", „Brand Registry"
- „PPC", „Sponsored Products", „ACoS", „TACoS", „Search Term Report", „Bid", „Negative Keywords"
- „Reviews", „BSR", „Best Sellers Rank"
- „Private Label", „Inventory", „COGS", „Margin"
- „Pan-EU", „CEE", „MCI"
- „FBA", „FBM", „MFN"

German FBA-Verkäufer don't say „Auflistung" for Listing or „Stichwort" for Keyword. Translating these reads as artificial and signals the writer doesn't know the German seller community.

### German-side terms that need to stay German
These are not Anglicisms — German sellers use the German term:
- „Mehrwertsteuer" / „USt." (VAT)
- „Steuerberater" (tax advisor — central role for any German seller)
- „Finanzamt" (tax office)
- „Kleinunternehmerregelung" (small business VAT exemption — very German-specific)
- „GmbH", „UG", „Einzelunternehmen" (legal entity types)
- „Gewerbeanmeldung" (business registration)
- „EORI-Nummer" (customs ID)
- „LUCID-Verpackungsregister" (German packaging law registration)
- „WEEE" / „Elektrogesetz" / „BattG" (electrical and battery recycling laws)
- „Versandverpackung" / „Verpackungslizenzierung"
- „Reverse-Charge-Verfahren"

### Tone of headlines
German blog headlines on technical topics tend to be *more declarative* than English. Where English uses „The Ultimate Guide", German prefers a clearer „Ratgeber" or just states the topic. Don't over-translate — write headlines that sound natural in German FBA content, not translated.

Examples that sound right:
- „Amazon FBA in Deutschland starten 2026 — der ehrliche Leitfaden"
- „Mehrwertsteuer für Amazon-Verkäufer: OSS, Pan-EU und der Steuerberater-Workflow"
- „Amazon.de vs. Amazon.com — fünf Unterschiede, die mich Geld gekostet haben"

Examples that sound translated:
- „Der ultimative Guide für Amazon FBA in Deutschland" (Anglicism + Superlativ — feels off)
- „Mehrwertsteuer entfesselt für Verkäufer" (translated „unlocked" — AI-smell)

## Anti-AI-smell list (Deutsch)
Avoid:
- „im stetigen Wandel", „ständig wachsenden Markt", „dynamische Landschaft"
- „nahtlos", „mühelos", „intuitiv"
- „Reise" als Metapher für Business-Aufbau
- „entfalten", „freisetzen", „revolutionieren"
- „auf das nächste Level bringen"
- „in der heutigen schnelllebigen Welt"
- „Tauche ein in…"

Use instead:
- Konkrete Zeitangaben: „seit 2018", „in den letzten zwei Jahren"
- Konkrete Zahlen: „19 %", „4-fache Verkäufe in 30 Tagen", „47 €"
- Konkrete Aktionen: „Lade den Bericht herunter", „Prüfe deine Margen"

## When to use English-language references

The articles link to the @AmazonFBAGirl YouTube channel — which is in English. Be transparent about it in German articles. Use a phrase like: „(Video auf Englisch — deutsche Untertitel folgen)" or „Hinweis: Die Video-Erklärung ist auf Englisch."

## Author byline

- Author name: **Ekaterina Rubtcova** (consistent with English content — confirmed with user)
- Self-reference in body: „Ich" — Katia speaks as the seller. Don't write „Frau Rubtcova schreibt…" — that breaks the voice.

## Final test for German content

Read the draft aloud. If it sounds like a translation, it is. Rewrite from the German perspective. The test: would a German FBA-Verkäuferin who reads `Amazon FBA Deutschland`-Foren or `Hörbar`-Podcasts recognize this voice as one of theirs? If yes, ship. If no, rewrite.
