# TheFBAGirl Brand Voice & Messaging

This document defines the TheFBAGirl brand voice, tone, and messaging framework. Reference this when writing all content to ensure consistency across blog, tutorials, reviews, news, and lifehacks.

TheFBAGirl is the content home for the **@AmazonFBAGirl** YouTube channel, hosted by **Katia**. Every piece of content must pair with or support the channel — blog posts should feel like the written sibling of a video, not a detached SEO artifact.

## Brand Voice Pillars

### 1. Honest-Operator Voice
- **What it means**: Katia writes from inside the business, not from the guru stage. She owns mistakes, discloses numbers, and tells readers what she'd do differently. No hype, no "6-figures-in-30-days" promises.
- **How it sounds**: First-person, experiential, calm. Claims are anchored to specific numbers, specific ASINs, specific months.
- **Example**: "I started on Amazon.de — looking back, I wish someone had given me the honest breakdown of what selling in Europe actually looks like."
- **Avoid**: Guru language ("hack the algorithm," "secret loophole," "passive income while you sleep"), fake urgency, motivational filler.

### 2. Numbers-First Clarity
- **What it means**: Every claim gets a number when possible. "More expensive" is weak; "$2.70 per cubic foot, up from $2.40" is the voice.
- **How it sounds**: Concrete, specific, benchmark-ready. Uses percentages, dollar figures, size tiers, time ranges.
- **Example**: "A 5% supplier discount on a $3.00 product saves you $0.15 per unit. Meanwhile, a product one inch too long might cost you $1.50 more per unit in fulfillment fees."
- **Avoid**: Vague modifiers (a lot, significantly, many sellers), unbacked superlatives, rounded-to-meaningless figures.

### 3. Mentor-to-Friend Tone
- **What it means**: Katia speaks as the slightly-more-experienced seller showing a peer how to avoid the mistakes she already made. Not a teacher lecturing from above, not a coach selling coaching.
- **How it sounds**: "You" and "I" (never "we at TheFBAGirl"). Short sentences for correction, longer ones for nuance. Admits uncertainty when it exists.
- **Example**: "Most sellers list features. Top sellers translate features into benefits with emotional hooks. Here's the simple pattern I use..."
- **Avoid**: "We believe...," royal-we corporate copy, condescension, talking down to beginners.

### 4. Practical-First, Theory-Second
- **What it means**: Every article gives the reader something to do this week. Frameworks only get introduced after the action is clear. No theory-for-theory's-sake.
- **How it sounds**: Opens with a scenario or a number, builds a specific step-by-step, ends with "download your Search Term Report for the last 60 days" — not "reflect on your journey."
- **Example**: "Download your Search Term Reports for the last 60 days and run through the optimization framework above. Identify your top 10 keywords by revenue and make sure each one has a dedicated exact match campaign."
- **Avoid**: Pure-theory articles, mindset-only posts without tactics, closing the article with rhetorical questions.

### 5. Community-Peer, Not Platform-Authority
- **What it means**: Katia is *a seller* who happens to teach, not a corporate brand. Content acknowledges when tools, Amazon rules, or markets changed — and admits when she's still figuring something out.
- **How it sounds**: Warm, slightly informal, sprinkles in "frankly," "honestly," "here's the uncomfortable truth." Never slick.
- **Example**: "Frankly, listing optimization is an area where Helium 10 is functional but not exceptional. The tools get the job done, but the interface feels dated."
- **Avoid**: PR-polished copy, "as industry leaders we...", over-qualified hedging, AI-smell phrases ("navigate," "unlock," "leverage," "ever-evolving landscape").

## Tone Guidelines

### General Tone
Imagine Katia is texting a seller friend who asked "is this worth doing?" She gives the honest answer, the numbers behind it, and what she'd do in their shoes. Confident but not cocky. Generous but not preachy. Practical but never dry.

### Tone Variations by Content Type

**Tutorials** — Instructive, step-by-step, patient.
- "Before building campaigns, you need to know what is available."
- "Here is the formula that actually tells you whether a product is profitable..."
- "Step 1: Check eligibility. You need to be enrolled in Amazon Brand Registry."

**Blog / Strategy posts** — Experienced, opinionated, anchored in numbers.
- "Here is the uncomfortable truth: your supplier cost is usually only 25-35% of your total COGS."
- "Most sellers target an ACoS between 15-30%, depending on their margin. But ACoS alone does not tell the full story."

**News** — Analytical, sober, action-oriented. Never breathless.
- "Amazon has officially rolled out its updated FBA fee schedule for 2026, and the changes are a mixed bag for sellers."
- "First, re-run your unit economics for every active SKU using the updated fee schedule."

**Reviews** — Fair, experiential, transparent about affiliate relationships.
- "After using Helium 10 extensively for over two years across multiple Amazon businesses, here is an honest assessment."
- "The pricing is higher than some competitors, but the breadth of tools justifies the cost if you use them."

**Lifehacks** — Direct, systems-oriented, light on fluff.
- "Stop checking Seller Central every 30 minutes. Instead, set two or three dedicated times per day."
- "If you do a task more than three times, document the steps."

## Messaging Framework

### Core Brand Messages

#### Message 1: Freedom Through Systems, Not Hustle
- **Concept**: Amazon FBA delivers real freedom — time, location, income — but only for sellers who build systems. Raw hustle burns out; systems scale.
- **Key Points**:
  - The goal is a business that runs without demanding 70-hour weeks.
  - Freedom comes from automation, SOPs, and clear unit economics — not from doing more.
  - Mindset matters, but it's downstream of correct fundamentals.
- **Usage**: Founder stories, lifehacks, "should I start FBA?" articles.

#### Message 2: Margin Is a Stack, Not a Number
- **Concept**: Profitability on Amazon is built from 8-10 line items (COGS, fulfillment, referral, storage, inbound placement, returns, PPC, shipping). Sellers who track only "supplier price" lose.
- **Key Points**:
  - Amazon fees are the biggest lever, not supplier price.
  - Dimensions and weight matter more than most sellers realize.
  - You can't improve what you don't measure per-SKU.
- **Usage**: Financial, sourcing, listing-optimization content.

#### Message 3: Start US, Expand Later
- **Concept**: Most new sellers should start on Amazon.com, not Europe or emerging marketplaces. Scale, review velocity, and tax simplicity all favor the US for a first product.
- **Key Points**:
  - Amazon.com dwarfs every individual European marketplace.
  - EU VAT compliance adds fixed overhead that crushes small sellers.
  - Expand to Europe *after* the US business is profitable and systemized.
- **Usage**: Beginner guides, marketplace-comparison posts, international-selling content.

#### Message 4: Honest Tool Reviews, No Sponsored Fluff
- **Concept**: Every tool review reflects hands-on use, real strengths, real weaknesses. Affiliate disclosure is explicit. No "best ever" unless it earns it.
- **Key Points**:
  - Tools are ranked against specific use cases, not universal "best."
  - Pricing is stated clearly; value is assessed per-tier.
  - Recommendations include "skip this if..." — an honest review rules some readers out.
- **Usage**: All review content, tool comparison articles, affiliate-disclosed posts.

#### Message 5: The Blog Is a Sibling to the Channel
- **Concept**: TheFBAGirl blog extends, references, and feeds the @AmazonFBAGirl YouTube channel. Each format serves readers who prefer that format — but they reinforce each other.
- **Key Points**:
  - Most blog posts embed or link the matching YouTube video.
  - CTAs drive newsletter signups and YouTube subscriptions — not course sales.
  - YouTube is the flagship; the site is where content lives, gets indexed, and gets searched.
- **Usage**: CTAs at the end of every article, author bios, social proof.

## Writing Style Guidelines

### Sentence Structure
- **Vary length**: Short punchy sentences for corrections and takeaways; longer explanatory sentences when unpacking a framework.
- **Active voice preferred**: "Amazon reduced fulfillment fees" — not "Fulfillment fees were reduced by Amazon."
- **Average length**: 15–20 words. Break anything over 25 words.
- **Clarity first**: If a reader has to re-read a sentence, rewrite it.

### Paragraph Structure
- **Length**: 2–4 sentences. Never a wall of text.
- **One idea per paragraph**: Each paragraph carries one point — use the next paragraph for the next one.
- **Transitions**: Use real transitions ("But here is the uncomfortable truth," "Here is the key insight") rather than stock ones ("Furthermore," "Moreover").
- **White space**: Mobile readers dominate. Keep paragraphs scannable.

### Word Choice
- **Specific over vague**: "$2.70 per cubic foot" beats "more expensive." "181 days" beats "after a while."
- **Verbs over nouns**: "Launch a product" beats "conduct a product launch."
- **Plain English over jargon**: Define any acronym on first use (ACoS, TACoS, BSR, A+ Content). Use the full phrase at least once.
- **Cut filler**: Delete "very," "really," "actually," "in order to," "it is important to note that."
- **No AI-smell words**: Avoid "leverage," "navigate," "unlock," "ever-evolving," "in today's landscape," "a myriad of," "delve into."

### Terminology

**Say This** → **Not That**
- Amazon seller → Amazon entrepreneur (too vague)
- FBA seller → FBA-preneur (cringe)
- Fulfillment fee → picking and packing cost (unclear)
- Referral fee → Amazon's cut (imprecise)
- Product research → niche hunting (vague)
- Search Term Report → STR (spell out on first use)
- Sponsored Products → SP ads (spell out on first use)
- Amazon.com → US Amazon (when contrast with Europe is needed)
- Seller Central → the Amazon dashboard (too generic)

## Content Formatting

### Headlines
- **Be specific**: "The Ultimate Amazon PPC Strategy Guide for 2026" > "PPC Tips."
- **Include the year** for time-sensitive topics (PPC, fees, product research methods).
- **Use numbers where they earn attention**: "7 Listing Optimization Tips That Actually Boost Sales" works; "Some Listing Tips" does not.
- **Under 70 characters** for the MDX `title` frontmatter (schema enforces it).
- **Emotional anchors allowed** — "The Brutal Truth," "The $0.50 Mistake" — when they reflect the content honestly.

### Subheadings (H2s)
- Descriptive and keyword-friendly. Mix declarative and question forms.
- Read as a table of contents that stands alone: "The True Cost Breakdown," "Why Virtual Bundles Work So Well," "Common PPC Mistakes."
- Never use "Section 1" or "Chapter Two" — always content-descriptive.

### Lists
- Bulleted for non-sequential items; numbered for steps or ranked priorities.
- Bold the first 2–4 words of each bullet when it helps scannability ("**Winning keywords** (low ACoS, high sales): Increase bids by 10-15%").
- Keep list items parallel — all complete sentences or all fragments, not mixed.

### Calls-to-Action
- **Default CTAs** in this order of preference:
  1. Subscribe to YouTube (@AmazonFBAGirl) — the flagship channel.
  2. Join the newsletter — for email list growth.
  3. Internal link to a related blog/tutorial — deep content engagement.
  4. Link to a recommended tool (with affiliate disclosure) — only when contextually honest.
- **Never** push a "course" or "coaching program" — TheFBAGirl does not sell one.
- CTAs should feel like a natural next step, not a sales pitch: "Watch the full breakdown in the video above" > "Click now for exclusive secrets!"

## Voice Examples

### Excellent TheFBAGirl Voice ✅

"You spent weeks negotiating your supplier down from $3.20 to $2.70 per unit. You feel great about that fifty-cent win. Then your first month of sales comes in and your profit is nowhere near what you projected.

Sound familiar? This is the single most common financial mistake I see Amazon sellers make, and it happens because we obsess over the one cost we can see — the purchase price — while ignoring the dozen costs that actually determine whether we make money.

A 5% supplier discount on a $3.00 product saves you $0.15 per unit. Meanwhile, a product that is one inch too long in one dimension might cost you $1.50 more per unit in fulfillment fees because it crosses a size tier threshold. Which problem deserves more of your attention?"

**Why this works**:
- Opens with a vivid, specific scenario that hooks the target reader instantly.
- Names the mistake without judgment ("we obsess") — inclusive peer voice.
- Immediately pivots to concrete numbers that reframe the problem.
- Ends with a rhetorical question that makes the point obvious without stating it.
- No guru language, no hype, no "I made $10K in my first week."

### Not TheFBAGirl Voice ❌

"In the ever-evolving landscape of e-commerce, savvy entrepreneurs are leveraging Amazon FBA to unlock passive income streams and build wealth. Our comprehensive guide will help you navigate the complexities of this dynamic marketplace, delving into proven strategies that have empowered countless sellers to achieve financial freedom.

Ready to embark on your Amazon journey? Join thousands of successful sellers who have transformed their lives through our battle-tested methodologies!"

**Why this fails**:
- "Ever-evolving landscape," "savvy," "leveraging," "unlock," "embark," "delve," "empowered," "battle-tested" — AI-smell bingo.
- Zero specifics: no numbers, no examples, no tactics.
- Fake community ("thousands of successful sellers") with no evidence.
- Guru promise ("financial freedom," "transform their lives") that nothing in the paragraph delivers.
- First-person-plural corporate voice ("our guide") instead of Katia's personal voice.

## Audience Understanding

### Who We Write For

**Primary Audience**: Aspiring and active Amazon FBA sellers in the US and EU, 25–45, serious about building a real business.
- Has saved or invested $3K–$50K they can put into inventory.
- Watches YouTube to learn; searches Google when stuck.
- Already sold on Amazon's opportunity; now wants to avoid specific mistakes.
- Technically comfortable (can install a Chrome extension, navigate Seller Central) but not a developer.
- Based in US, UK, Germany, or non-English-speaking Europe.

**Secondary Audiences**:
- **YouTube subscribers**: Already fans of the channel; visit the site for deeper reading and referenced resources.
- **E-commerce-curious**: Researching whether FBA is right for them; might convert to primary audience.
- **Scaling sellers**: Have one or more products live; looking for specific optimizations and tool reviews.
- **Industry/news readers**: Following Amazon policy changes and marketplace trends even if they don't sell.

### What They Care About

**Top Priorities**:
1. Not losing money on their first/next product launch.
2. Understanding true unit economics (profit after all fees, not revenue).
3. PPC that actually converts without burning the budget.
4. Which tools are worth paying for (and which aren't).
5. Staying ahead of Amazon's policy and fee changes.

**Pain Points**:
1. Information overload: every guru sells the "one true method."
2. Outdated tutorials: tactics that worked in 2020 don't work in 2026.
3. Hidden costs: fees, returns, and storage that destroy margin models.
4. Tool subscription bloat: paying $300+/month for overlapping tools.
5. PPC feels like a slot machine: spend $1K, get $500 back.

### How to Serve Them

- **Respect their time**: The answer in the first 100 words. Hook, then expand.
- **Show the math**: Fee changes, PPC benchmarks, and ROI calculations get actual numbers.
- **Acknowledge uncertainty**: "What worked for me" > "the only way." Let readers adapt.
- **Connect to the channel**: Every article should make the YouTube video feel like the natural next step (or the article is the natural next step *from* the video).
- **Never sell a course**: Trust compounds when the content is free and the CTAs are honest.

## Quality Checklist

Before publishing any content, verify:

- [ ] **Voice**: Reads like Katia, not like a SaaS marketing team.
- [ ] **Numbers**: Key claims are anchored to specific dollar amounts, percentages, or timeframes.
- [ ] **First-person**: "I" and "you" — never "we" as the brand.
- [ ] **Named sources**: Amazon announcements, studies, or tools linked or cited when referenced.
- [ ] **YouTube connection**: The article embeds, links, or references the matching YouTube video when one exists.
- [ ] **Honest CTAs**: No hype, no fake urgency, no promises of "passive income."
- [ ] **No AI-smell**: Zero "leverage," "navigate," "unlock," "ever-evolving."
- [ ] **Scannable**: Paragraphs 2–4 sentences; H2s work as a table of contents.
- [ ] **Action for the reader**: Clear "do this week" takeaway.
- [ ] **Affiliate disclosure** if any tool link is monetized.
